    The web phone¶

    The Overview tab is not a status page that happens to mention calls. It is a handset: a keypad, a three-button call rail, a flash key, a microphone mute and two level meters, carrying the same line the RJ11 socket does.

    With nothing plugged into the RJ11 socket, this is a complete telephone. That is not a fallback: the line is carried by the modem, and the page is one of the three things that can hold it.

    The page must be open over HTTPS

    A browser takes no microphone from a page it does not trust, and it sends audio to an encrypted socket only from an encrypted page. Open LuCI over https://.

    The controls¶

    control what it does
    the number field what you are dialing. Up to 32 digits, with a backspace that deletes the last one
    the keypad 1–9, *, 0, # with their letter groups, and 0 giving + for an international prefix
    Call / Answer one button, in one slot. It is Call on an idle line and Answer while a call rings
    Flash the handset's flash key. Hold, swap, take the waiting call, take a held call back — and a digit typed after it is a code
    Hang up / Reject / Cancel the same slot again: Hang up in a call, Reject while a call rings, Cancel while your own attempt is still setting up
    Use this computer's microphone and speaker opens the audio session. Without it the call exists but you cannot hear it
    Mute / Unmute suspends the microphone alone. You still hear the caller
    Stop audio closes the session. The call goes on; the page just stops carrying it
    the level meters two live bars, so you can see both directions of audio while the call is up
    Technical details a folded row of counters: who owns the answer, the call revision, frames to the audio side, frames from it, frames dropped

    A control that cannot act right now keeps its place and is disabled rather than hidden, so the layout never jumps under your cursor:

    when what is disabled
    a call is ringing in, or your own attempt is setting up, or the call is being torn down the keypad
    nothing is active, held or waiting Flash
    the handset, the answering machine or another front end already holds the answer Answer
    no audio session exists yet Mute and Stop audio are not shown at all

    Flash, and the codes that follow it¶

    The page's Flash button stands in for a physical hook break, and the codes are the handset's:

    after the flash what happens
    1 end the current call and take the other one
    2 hold this call and take the other; with two live calls it swaps them, with one held call it takes it back
    3 join the two calls into a three-way conversation. Needs 3-way calling switched on, which it is not as installed
    0 refuse the waiting call, or release the held one

    The window for the digit is three seconds, and the page says so under the keypad while it is open. This is the same window the handset's flash key uses, described on the handset page.

    Who owns the line¶

    Two rules hold the whole thing together, and both are visible in the log.

    The handset wins a lift. Lifting the handset during a page call carries the call on at the phone. The log records the handset as the one that answered it, that call's card says so, and the page's microphone and speaker stop with it. A call the page put on hold is not taken by a lift: the lift gets the recall dial tone and the held call stays the page's.

    One owner of the playback ring. Exactly one thing may send audio toward the caller at a time: nothing, the greeting, a network phone, or this page. The handset sits on the same ladder, which is why a lift ends a browser session cleanly instead of fighting it.

    The page cannot take a call the handset already holds, and it never interrupts a call in progress. What its Answer button can take over is the call the answering machine is holding — and taking it that way ends the message at that moment.

    The certificate step, once¶

    The audio stream arrives on its own port, and a browser will not accept a self-signed certificate silently on a port it has not seen before. So the page shows one button:

    Open the audio address (one time)

    Press it, accept the browser's warning, close that tab. The browser then remembers the router's certificate for the audio port and the step stays out of the way forever after. Until you do it, the page says so plainly rather than letting you press Use this computer's microphone and wonder why nothing happens.

    What the audio path is¶

    How the browser's voice reaches the line

    One HTTP request becomes a WebSocket on the router's LAN address, port 7443 by default (web_port, web_iface), carrying this daemon's own frames: twelve header bytes — marker, version, type, sequence number, timestamp — followed by 160 samples of 16-bit linear audio in network byte order, which is 20 ms at 8 kHz. At 16 kHz the same path carries two time samples per frame, packed the way the kernel reads them, so a wideband call is not stretched.

    Admission is a token minted by the daemon for the call revision the page is looking at: single use, valid thirty seconds, sent in the first frame of the encrypted stream — never in a URL, never in a log, never in a form field. A refused page is told which fixed reason applied: refused-busy, refused-nocall, refused-revision, refused-mode, refused-rate, refused-port, refused-object, dropped-slow.

    The stream is TLS on the router's own HTTPS certificate pair, which is /etc/uhttpd.crt and /etc/uhttpd.key unless you point it elsewhere.

    Why this is not WebRTC. Three reasons, and they are all properties of this router rather than preferences: the audio has to land sample-exactly inside the kernel's audio bridge and the recording mirror, which a jitter buffer will not do; the allowed library set for this code stops short of ICE, SRTP and Opus; and the peer is always on your own LAN, where there is no NAT to traverse, no jitter to absorb and no codec negotiation to lose. A LAN stream that is 20 ms late is broken, not adaptively degraded.

    What you hear from the page¶

    • your own ring: a 1 kHz double burst every four seconds while a call rings, started only after you have interacted with the page, because a browser refuses sound until then;
    • progress tones from the line itself, in the call audio;
    • nothing at all if the audio session is not open — a call can be up with the page showing its state and no sound, which is the Stop audio state, not a fault.

    When the page's phone misbehaves¶

    symptom what it means
    Use this computer's microphone does nothing the browser refused the microphone, or the page is not over HTTPS
    The button is there but audio never starts the certificate step has not been done in this browser. Press Open the audio address (one time)
    You dial and the call sets up, but you hear nothing the audio session is not open, or it was stopped. The meters being flat both ways is the tell
    You hear the caller and they cannot hear you the microphone is muted, or the browser revoked it mid-call
    Answer is greyed out while a call rings the handset is already off hook, or another front end holds the answer. That is the ownership rule, not a bug
    The page says the phone side is not running the daemon is down. The card offers the Health tab, where you can start it
    A call the page held is not taken by lifting the handset correct: a lift gets the recall dial tone, and the page's held call stays the page's

    Questions people ask¶

    Can I make phone calls without an analog handset on the AW1000?

    Yes. With nothing in the RJ11 socket the AWvoice page is the telephone: dial from its keypad, answer with its Answer button, hang up, mute, and send DTMF. A soft phone on your own network is a third option once the SIP switch is on.

    Does the browser phone use WebRTC?

    No. It uses an encrypted WebSocket on the LAN carrying this daemon's own 20 ms audio frames, admitted by a single-use token. The reason is that the audio has to be placed sample-exactly into the kernel bridge and the recording mirror, and a LAN peer needs none of what WebRTC exists for.

    Why does my browser ask for a certificate warning just for audio?

    The audio stream is a second port with the router's own self-signed certificate. Accepting it once, through the page's Open the audio address button, is the whole setup.

    Can two people use the phone at once — the handset and the page?

    One of them owns the audio at a time. Lifting the handset during a page call carries the call on at the phone and stops the page's microphone and speaker.
